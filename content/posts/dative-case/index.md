## Dative suffixes: a, e, ya, ye

The Turkish dative case usually means **to** or **toward**. It shows the direction of movement, the target of an action, or the person/thing something is given or said to.

### Which form do I use?

The form depends on two things:

1. Does the word end in a **consonant** or a **vowel**?
2. Is the last vowel in the word a **back vowel** (`a, ı, o, u`) or a **front vowel** (`e, i, ö, ü`)?

| Word ends with | Last vowel is              | Use   | Example                       |
| :------------- | :------------------------- | :---- | :---------------------------- |
| consonant      | back vowel (`a, ı, o, u`)  | `-a`  | `okul` → `okula`              |
| consonant      | front vowel (`e, i, ö, ü`) | `-e`  | `ev` → `eve`                  |
| vowel          | back vowel (`a, ı, o, u`)  | `-ya` | `araba` → `arabaya`           |
| vowel          | front vowel (`e, i, ö, ü`) | `-ye` | `üniversite` → `üniversiteye` |

### Basic examples

Üniversite**ye** gidiyorum. → I am going to the university. \
Öğrenci öğretmen**e** soru soruyor. → The student asks the teacher a question. \
Konser**e** gidiyorum. → I am going to a concert. \
Araba**ya** biniyorum. → I am getting into the car. \
Okul**a** gidiyoruz. → We are going to school.
